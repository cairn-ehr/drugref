About 26 years ago, a group of clinicians in Australia, Gemanym and Canada started a project called "drugref.org" - aiming to produc e a comprehensive pharmacological reference database for clinical use without access barriers and paywalls. The project died for lack of volunteer contributors, parts of it surviving in projects such as the Canadian OSCAR EHR software.

Almost 3 decades later, there is still no comparable clinically focused pharmaceutical reference database freely available. The issue is the clinical relevance of the information provided - what a clinician needs to know at the time of prescription is not just information about indications, contraindications, interactions, half life and elinination pathways, compatibility with pregnancy, breastfeeding, chronic diseases such as renal or hepatic failure, incompatibility with certain genotypes or ethnicities - but also how clinically relevant that information actually is. When comparing a variety of sources, there is often a bewildering discrepancy and difficulty in chasing down the evidence based justification for each statement - if there even is one.

Drugref V2 has become possible due to the assistance of AI agents able to scour the available literature for evidence at a tiny fration of the cost of huan labour. Instead of dozens or even hundreds of experienced clinicians spending years going through millions of publications, suitably harnessed AI agents can do the work in weeks at a fraction of the cost of employing een a single clinician for the purpose. The skill of expensive and scarce skilled clinicians can be delegated to verificion and curation of the collected information.

What Drugref V2 aims to provide is exactly the information a clinican needs at the time of prescribiong or eviewing a drug chart: Is this drug indicated for the purpose, what dose might be suitable for that particular patient, is it compatible with both the concomittant other medication and supplements and dietary habits as well as with concomittant illness or genetic specifities - and the patient's situation in life such as pregnancy, breastfeeding, or infancy? It needs to be provided in a form suitable for algorithms and/or AI agents to automatically double check on the decision process and flag potentially harmful constellations - based on actual clinical relevance in the context, rather than the often useless or even harmful flooding with clinically irrelevant "warnings" most of us senior cinicans have experienced as a leading cause for click-through fatigue. It needs to be available globally regardless of legislation, regulation framework, or financial ability.
